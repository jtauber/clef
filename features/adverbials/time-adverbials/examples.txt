They arrived [yesterday].
We will return [tomorrow].
She left [early] [today].
    Two adverbial words count separately.
[Afterwards], we went home.
Try [again].
    Again expresses repetition.
I saw her [once].
    Once expresses the number of occasions.
The work will finish very [soon].
    Only the time adverbial is marked; very modifies it.
She is working [now].
He had [previously] worked as a teacher.
We can discuss it [later].
They have [already] left.
    Already is a time adverbial absent from Biber's list.
We met [then].
    Temporal then is absent from Biber's list.
The {early} train was cancelled.
    Early modifies a noun.
{Tomorrow} is a holiday.
    Tomorrow is the subject, not an adverbial.
{Once} you arrive, call me.
    Once introduces a temporal clause.
We met on Monday.
    The prepositional phrase expresses time but is outside this feature's scope.
They worked all night.
    The noun phrase expresses duration but is outside this feature's scope.
