# Nominalizations

Nouns formed from verbs or adjectives with a nominalizing suffix to name an action, process, state or property, such as *destruction*, *movement* and *kindness*.

## Conventions

- Count each nominalization word once, including plural forms.
- Include nominalizations with suffixes outside Biber's four families, such as *arrival* and *refusal*. These are part of the feature but fall outside his detector.
- Treat nominal activity and event uses of participle forms under gerunds. Lexical *-ing* nouns naming objects or results, such as *a building*, belong to other nouns.
- Exclude nouns formed without a nominalizing suffix, such as *a reply* and *an exchange*. This catalogue treats them as other nouns.
- A matching ending alone does not establish a nominalization: *cement*, *moment* and *city* are not included merely because their spelling fits the rule.
- Exclude verbal uses, proper names and mentions of word forms. Mark only the nominalization word, leaving determiners, modifiers and complements outside it.

## Notes

Feature 14 in section E of [@biber_1988, p. 227]. The printed rule counts words ending in *-tion*, *-ment*, *-ness* and *-ity*, including their plural forms. It does not require a noun tag. The Biber rule preserves this form-based test, with case-insensitive matching and plural endings *-tions*, *-ments*, *-nesses* and *-ities*.

The linguistic definition is broader than that suffix inventory. Conversely, the rule can count unrelated words, verbs and proper names with the same endings. It operates on supplied tokens, so tokenization can affect compounds and possessives. These limitations belong to the detection method; the examples retain the linguistic distinctions.
