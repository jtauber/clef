# Pronoun it

The pronoun *it*, whether it refers to an entity or situation or serves a grammatical function without a referent, as in *it is raining*.

## Conventions

- Subject and object uses count: *it fell*, *I found it*.
- References to people, animals, things and whole situations count.
- Dummy uses count, including weather and time expressions, anticipatory *it* in *it is useful to practise*, and cleft *it* in *it was Lee who called*.
- Contracted forms count once. Mark only the pronoun part: *[It]'s*, *[It]’ll*.
- Possessive *its* and reflexive *itself* are outside this feature's scope.
- Abbreviations and mentions of word forms do not count: *IT support*, *it* named as a word.

## Notes

Feature 9 in [@biber_1988, pp. 225–226]. CLEF treats this as the form *it*, rather than the whole *it* paradigm.

The entry gives no token-level algorithm. The biber rule reconstructs it through case-insensitive form matching, with common auxiliary contractions handled as in the C1 rules. It does not distinguish pronouns from abbreviations or mentions of the word form.
