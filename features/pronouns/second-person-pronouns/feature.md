# Second-person pronouns

Personal-pronoun forms used to address a person or group, including possessive and reflexive forms and generic uses of *you*.

## Conventions

- Singular and plural *you* count in both subject and object positions.
- Dependent and independent possessives count: *your book*, *the book is yours*.
- Reflexive and emphatic uses count: *help yourself*, *you yourselves checked it*.
- Generic *you* counts: *you cannot predict the weather*.
- Contracted forms count once. Mark the pronoun part: *[You]'re*, *[You]'ve*.
- Archaic second-person forms count, including *thou*, *thee*, *thy*, *thine* and *thyself*.
- Mentions of word forms do not count: *you* named as a word.

## Notes

Feature 7 in [@biber_1988, p. 225]. The printed inventory omits *yours*, which counts under CLEF's conventions.

The biber rule uses word forms without a POS test. It recognises the pronoun as a separate token or fused with common contracted auxiliaries. It cannot distinguish a use of a pronoun from a mention of its word form.

The MAT reconstruction adds *thou*, *thy*, *thee* and *thyself* to the four forms in Biber's printed inventory. It still omits *yours* and *thine*. It uses MAT's word-boundary matching without a pronoun-tag test and counts split YOU contractions, but supplies no expansion of fused contractions.
