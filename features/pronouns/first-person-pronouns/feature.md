# First-person pronouns

Personal-pronoun forms referring to the speaker or writer, alone or as part of a group, including possessive and reflexive forms.

## Conventions

- Singular and plural forms count: *I*, *me*, *we* and *us*.
- Dependent and independent possessives count: *my book*, *our books*, *the book is mine*, *the books are ours*.
- Reflexive and emphatic uses count: *I hurt myself*, *we ourselves checked it*.
- First-person forms count in quotations and in uses such as editorial *we*.
- Contracted forms count once. Mark the pronoun part: *[I]'m*, *[We]'ve*. In *let's*, mark contracted *us*: *Let['s] begin*.
- Homographs and mentions of word forms do not count: the abbreviation *US*, the Roman numeral *I*, or *I* named as a word.

## Notes

Feature 6 in [@biber_1988, p. 225]. The printed inventory omits *mine* and *ours*, which count under CLEF's conventions.

The biber rule uses word forms without a POS test. Its contraction patterns accept separate pronoun tokens and common fused spellings; they are an interpretation of the source's contraction instruction. Case-insensitive matching also counts the abbreviation *US* and the numeral *I*.

The MAT reconstruction uses the same eight base forms, with MAT's word-boundary matching and no pronoun-tag requirement. Unlike the biber rule, it supplies no contraction expansion: a separately tokenized I or WE counts, but a fused contraction and contracted US in *let's* do not. Its notes distinguish the tagging pass from MAT's raw-text preprocessing.
