# Possessive and obligation HAVE GOT

HAVE GOT expressing possession or obligation: *I've got a car* or *she's got to leave*.

## Conventions

- Count each HAVE GOT construction once, including contracted, negative and inverted forms.
- Include possession broadly, such as relationships, attributes and problems, as well as ownership.
- Include obligation HAVE GOT TO.
- Exclude perfect GET describing acquisition, an event or a change of state: *has got much better* and *has got arrested* do not count.
- Exclude *have gotten* and HAVE without GOT.
- Mark HAVE and GOT. Leave subjects, adverbs, possessions and the following TO-infinitive outside the marked pieces. Use one shared label when HAVE and GOT are separated.
- Mark unresolved possession/acquisition readings as unresolved unless context or an example note fixes the intended reading.

## Notes

The contextual rule draws on MFTE's separate HAVE GOT category [@mfte] and anchors on GOT. It excludes GOT immediately followed by another participle, but does not otherwise distinguish possession or obligation from acquisition and change of state. Its short POS-based context window can miss interrupted constructions.
