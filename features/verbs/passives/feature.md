# Passives

Passive predications formed with BE or GET, where the subject corresponds to an object of the corresponding active verb.

## Conventions

- Include the occurrences defined by both the BE-passive and GET-passive features, with finite or non-finite auxiliaries, contractions, perfects and progressives.
- Count each coordinated passive predicate separately, including when an auxiliary is shared.
- Exclude adjectival and ordinary copular uses, HAVE/GET causatives and reduced participial modifiers without BE or GET. Mark unresolved verbal/adjectival readings as unresolved.
- Mark the passive BE or GET and its passive participle. Leave earlier perfect HAVE, subjects, adverbs and complements outside the marked pieces.
- With a shared auxiliary, mark it with the first coordinated participle and mark later passive participles as separate occurrences.
- Evaluate the broader passive feature independently; do not add its count to the counts of its subtypes as though they were separate occurrences.

## Notes

This feature supplies the shared broader category for BE passives and GET passives under CLEF's conventions. The `subsumed_by` declarations on those features describe their linguistic scope. The contextual rule separately chooses to combine their contextual detectors. It inherits their detection limitations, including causative and adjectival false positives and possible matches across clause boundaries. Subsumption itself does not require any particular detector or rule composition.
