# By-passives

Passive predications formed with BE or GET in which a by-phrase expresses the participant corresponding to the subject of the active construction, as in *the parcel was delivered by a courier*.

## Conventions

- Include finite and non-finite BE and GET passives, contractions, perfects and progressives, following the broader passive feature.
- Include non-human agents and causes as well as human agents: *the house was destroyed by fire* counts.
- Include agentive by-phrases separated from the participle by other material or placed before the clause, including *by whom was it written?*.
- Exclude temporal, spatial, instrumental and manner uses of *by* when they do not express the corresponding active subject. Those passives remain agentless unless another by-phrase supplies an agent.
- Count each coordinated passive predicate separately. A shared agentive by-phrase can apply to more than one predicate.
- Exclude active clauses, HAVE/GET causatives, adjectival and ordinary copular uses, and reduced participle clauses or modifiers without BE or GET. Mark unresolved verbal/adjectival readings as unresolved.
- Mark the passive BE or GET and its passive participle. Leave the by-phrase, subjects, adverbs, complements and earlier auxiliaries outside the marked pieces; in *has been being watched*, mark only *being watched*.
- With a shared auxiliary, mark it with the first passive participle and mark subsequent passive participles as separate occurrences.

## Notes

Feature 18 in section F of [@biber_1988, p. 228]. It classifies passive predications by the presence of an agentive by-phrase. The definition includes both BE and GET passives, so its declared broader feature is passives.

The Biber rule adds an immediately following *by* to each of the two printed BE patterns. The anchor remains the participle; the by-phrase provides classification context. Its notes describe the mapping to supplied Penn tags and the treatment of bare *be*.

The detector does not analyse the role of *by* or require its complement. It can count *was delivered by noon* and miss *was delivered yesterday by a courier*. GET passives, some questions and coordinated predicates without a repeated BE also fall outside its patterns. The original tagging and manual correction of participle functions [@biber_1988, pp. 216–217] are not reproduced.
